# C — Combine

**Definition.** Merge two or more elements, ideas, purposes, products, audiences, formats, partners or steps into one.

**The move.** This is conceptual blending. Koestler called it "bisociation": connecting two frames that are normally separate. The further apart the two frames, the more original the blend. The more a real need connects them, the more useful the blend.

## Trigger questions
- What about a blend, an alloy, an assortment, an ensemble? (Osborn)
- Which units could be combined? (Osborn)
- Which purposes could be combined? (Osborn)
- Which appeals could be combined, for example emotional and rational, or fun and useful? (Osborn)
- Which features of two different products could merge?
- What could be bundled with this?
- Which two audiences could be served at once?
- Which partner, brand, creator or expert could we team up with?
- Which unrelated field could be fused with this one?
- What if two steps became one?
- What could be combined to lower cost or create synergy?
- "What if this were also a ___?"
- Which two of our *own* ideas from this session could merge? Use this one at the convergence stage.

## By domain
- **Content / marketing:**
  - An infographic plus a long-form post; a guide plus a downloadable template.
  - Co-branded campaigns.
  - An evergreen piece combined with a news hook.
  - Two personas merged into one story.
  - A product launch combined with a cause.
- **Writing:**
  - Genre mashups, such as *Pride and Prejudice and Zombies*.
  - Merge two minor characters into one stronger one.
  - Braid two timelines.
  - Open a piece with a statistic and an anecdote together.
  - A headline that is also the hook of the first line.
- **Design / UI:**
  - Search combined with commands (a command palette).
  - Reviews inside the product description.
  - Merged settings pages.
  - A logo that is also the product shape.
  - An empty state that is also the onboarding.
- **Business / process:**
  - Merge two handoffs or review stages.
  - Cross-train roles.
  - Bundle services.
  - Combine customer support with product research.

## Pitfalls
- Swiss-army bloat: combining features that no user actually needs together.
- Blends that dilute a clear value proposition. Ask what the *one* reason to choose this is after the merge.
- Only combining things that are already adjacent, which is safe but rarely fresh.

## Verified or well-documented examples
- The iPhone. Jobs introduced it in 2007 as an iPod, a phone and an internet communicator in one device.
- Reese's Peanut Butter Cups; the clock radio; the camera phone.
- Avoid the claim that the Gutenberg press was "wine press + coin punch". It oversimplifies a more complex adaptation.
