# P — Put to other uses

**Definition.** Find new uses, users, markets or contexts for the thing, either as it is or after a small change. This includes turning waste, by-products, leftovers or data into something valuable.

**The move.** Remap the function: ask "what else is this good for?" instead of "what is this for?". It is the reverse of functional fixedness.

## Trigger questions
- Are there new ways to use it as it is? (Osborn)
- What other uses would it have if modified? (Osborn)
- Who else could use it: a child, an older person, a professional, a beginner, a different culture?
- How might people with different abilities use it?
- Which other market, industry or department could want it?
- What could the by-product, leftover, offcut or waste become?
- What could the data, archive or "exhaust" from this process become?
- Where else could it be used: another room, platform, country or occasion?
- What happens if it is used "wrongly" on purpose?
- What problem is it accidentally good at solving?
- Could a failure, a discontinued product or abandoned work become something else?

## By domain
- **Content / marketing:**
  - A webinar into a blog series; a blog post into a social carousel series.
  - Internal docs or sales FAQs into public content.
  - Customer data into an annual "state of" report.
  - Reviews into ad copy.
  - Backlist titles into themed reading lists or gift bundles.
- **Writing:**
  - A cut scene into a short story or bonus content.
  - A character's backstory into marketing material.
  - An article's sidebar into a standalone piece.
  - Research notes into a newsletter.
  - A manuscript's world into a guide, map or companion.
- **Design / UI:**
  - Empty states as onboarding.
  - Error pages as brand moments.
  - Loading time as tips.
  - Design-system components reused for marketing pages.
  - An illustration set turned into merchandise.
- **Business / process:**
  - Sell or open-source internal tools.
  - Rent out idle assets and off-peak capacity.
  - Turn complaint data into a product roadmap.
  - Turn training material into a paid course.

## Pitfalls
- Uses that no market actually wants. Name the user who would pay for it, or care about it.
- Repurposing that confuses the brand.
- Lazy repurposing: the same content pasted into a new channel without adapting it to how that channel works. Combine this letter with Adapt.

## Verified or well-documented examples
- **Play-Doh.** It started as a wallpaper cleaner. Teacher Kay Zufall suggested turning it into a toy, and it relaunched in 1956 (Smithsonian).
- **Bubble Wrap.** It started in 1957 as an attempt at textured wallpaper. Greenhouse insulation failed before packaging succeeded (Smithsonian).
- **Post-it Notes.** Spencer Silver's weak adhesive, which Art Fry used as a bookmark.
- **Kleenex**, a cold-cream remover remarketed as a handkerchief. **Listerine**, a surgical antiseptic that became a mouthwash. **Slack**, which grew from an internal tool at a games company. **Sildenafil**, an angina candidate repurposed.
- Avoid these: "AWS was Amazon selling spare capacity" (Amazon has denied it), and Carver's "300 peanut uses" as commercial products.
