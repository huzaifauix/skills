# R — Reverse / Rearrange

**Definition.**
- **Rearrange:** change the order, sequence, layout, pattern, pace or schedule, or swap components around.
- **Reverse:** invert it. The opposite, backwards, upside down, inside out; roles swapped; cause and effect swapped.

Some versions of SCAMPER use only one of the two. Use both. (A version with both, plus a split M, is sometimes called "SCAMMPERR".)

**The move.** Invert assumptions and search through possible orderings. Every category has invisible defaults, and reversing them makes those defaults visible.

## Trigger questions
**Rearrange**
- Could the components be interchanged? (Osborn)
- Another pattern, layout or sequence? (Osborn)
- Could cause and effect be transposed? (Osborn)
- Change the pace or schedule: earlier, later, all at once, in pieces? (Osborn)
- What if the last step came first?
- What if the order were chosen by the user?

**Reverse**
- Transpose positive and negative? (Osborn)
- What is the opposite? (Osborn)
- Turn it backwards, upside down, inside out? (Osborn)
- Reverse the roles: customer and provider, teacher and student, reader and author? (Osborn)
- What if we did the opposite of the category norm?
- What if the problem were the solution, or the weakness the selling point?
- **Reverse brainstorm.** How would we make this *worse*? List the answers, then invert each one into an idea.

## By domain
- **Content / marketing:**
  - Lead with the conclusion.
  - Publish the "what we got wrong" post.
  - Let customers write the ad, or let readers pick the cover.
  - Run anti-category campaigns: "don't buy this", an anti-Black-Friday campaign.
  - Release the ending first.
  - Reveal the price at the start.
- **Writing:**
  - Tell the story from the antagonist's or a minor character's side. *Wicked*; *Wide Sargasso Sea*; *Rosencrantz and Guildenstern Are Dead*.
  - Reverse chronology, as in *Memento*.
  - Start in medias res.
  - Swap problem and solution in copy; pose the headline as the reader's objection; make the villain sympathetic and the hero suspect.
- **Design / UI:**
  - Let people try before they sign up, so value comes before commitment.
  - Flip the visual hierarchy.
  - Design dark-first.
  - Reverse the card order or reading pattern.
  - Mirror the layout for right-to-left languages.
  - Put the outcome above the form.
- **Business / process:**
  - Pull instead of push.
  - Pay first, or pay later.
  - Customer self-service.
  - Test-first instead of test-last.
  - A pre-mortem instead of a post-mortem.
  - The customer sets the price.

## Pitfalls
- Novelty for its own sake. A reversal should expose a better assumption, not just look odd.
- Reversing conventions people rely on to find their way, such as navigation placement or reading order, without testing.
- Treating it as a gimmick. Name the assumption you flipped and why the flip helps.

## Verified or well-documented examples
- **Piggly Wiggly (1916).** Clarence Saunders reversed the clerk-fetches-goods model into self-service, patented as a "Self-Serving Store" in 1917.
- **Heinz Easy Squeeze (2002).** An upside-down bottle with a valve.
- Ford's assembly line inverted the meatpackers' *dis*assembly line, and moved the work to the worker instead of the worker to the work.
- Avoid the claim that Columbus "proved the Earth was round". It is a myth.
