# S — Substitute

**Definition.** Swap one element for another while keeping the rest. The element can be a component, material, person, place, channel, process, power source, rule, time, name, or tone.

**The move.** This letter breaks *functional fixedness*. First name the job the element does, then ask what else could do that job. If you substitute without naming the job, you get either cosmetic change or something broken.

## Trigger questions
- Who else could do this, say it, own it, or be featured in it?
- What else could go here instead?
- What other material, ingredient or medium could be used?
- What other process or method would get the same result?
- What other power source, engine or incentive could drive it?
- What other place or channel could it happen in?
- What other time could it happen: season, time of day, stage of the journey?
- What other tone of voice could it use? (Osborn)
- Can the rules be changed? (Michalko)
- Can the name be changed?
- What would a cheaper, faster, greener or more familiar stand-in be?
- What assumption is keeping this part in place, and what if it were false?
- What is the *function* of this part, and what is the most unexpected thing that could perform it?

## By domain
- **Content / marketing:**
  - Swap the format: text to video, carousel, quiz, interactive tool, audio.
  - Swap the spokesperson: brand to customer, employee, critic or author.
  - Swap the channel, the CTA verb, the offer (discount to exclusive access), or the proof (claims to a live demo).
- **Writing:**
  - Swap the protagonist, the point of view, the setting, the era, or the genre. *West Side Story* moves *Romeo and Juliet* to 1950s New York.
  - Swap the narrator's reliability.
  - In copy, swap an abstract noun for a concrete image, a feature for a benefit, or a statement for a question.
- **Design / UI:**
  - Swap icons for labels or labels for icons; a photo for an illustration; the typeface or palette.
  - Swap a modal for inline editing, a password for a magic link or passkey, a form for a conversation.
- **Business / process:**
  - Async written updates instead of a stand-up.
  - Automation instead of a manual approval.
  - A subscription instead of a one-off sale.
  - A different supplier, material or pricing unit.

## Pitfalls
- Like-for-like swaps, such as one stock photo for another. Nothing changes.
- Substituting without asking what function the original served, which breaks the thing.
- Swapping only surface elements (colour, name) and never the deeper ones (who, where, when, by what process).

## Verified or well-documented examples
- Plant-protein meat analogues substitute the protein source while keeping the eating experience.
- Michalko's example: a paperclip maker switched to coloured plastic, so the clips could also colour-code documents. Treat this as an illustrative anecdote.
- Self-checkout substitutes the customer for the cashier. This overlaps with Reverse.
