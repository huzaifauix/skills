# Pillars and topics

## Worked example (from the framework)

**Category:** Communication Coaching

| Pillar | Topics |
|---|---|
| Speaking | Storytelling, Openings, Slide design, Q&A handling, Pacing, Pauses, Filler words, Body language, Vocal variety, Stage fright, Eye contact, Closing lines, Pitching, Virtual talks |
| Conversations | Active listening, Giving feedback, Saying no, Conflict, Negotiation, Small talk, Boundaries, Tough emails, Good questions, Tone of voice, Disagreeing, Apologizing, Salary talks, Interruptions |
| Confidence | Self-doubt, Imposter feelings, Nerves, Authenticity, Presence, Clarity, Vulnerability, Reframing, Self-talk, Composure, Rehearsing, Introversion, Charisma, Small wins |

Example post: **Confidence** + **Imposter feelings** + **Failure story** → *"The meeting where I froze."*

Notice the shape: two pillars are skills the client is buying (Speaking, Conversations), and one is the inner obstacle that stops them (Confidence). Topics are short, concrete, and each one could carry a dozen posts.

## Good pillars

A pillar set is ready when every pillar passes all of these:

- **Audience-first.** Named for something the audience wants or struggles with, not a department of the business. *Cash flow* beats *Our accounting services*.
- **Deep.** You can list at least 10 distinct topics under it without stretching.
- **Distinct.** A given topic clearly belongs to one pillar. If many topics could sit in two pillars, merge or redraw them.
- **Tied to the offer.** Someone who reads a lot of this pillar becomes more likely to need what the user sells.
- **Ownable.** The user has the experience or point of view to talk about it credibly, based on `profile.md`.

Useful mix for three pillars: one on the **core problem** the offer solves, one on an **adjacent skill or situation** the audience faces around it, and one on the **mindset, belief or behind-the-scenes** side, where the user's personality and point of view show. Use this as a starting shape, not a rule.

## Sources for topics

Pull topics from several of these so the list isn't all one kind:

- **Questions** the audience asks before buying, and while using the product or service
- **Mistakes** they commonly make, and the **myths** they believe
- **Moments**: specific situations they find themselves in (*the first hire*, *tax deadline week*, *the client who ghosts*)
- **Tools, methods and terms** in the field
- **Objections** to buying, and the **alternatives** they consider
- **Outcomes** they want, and the **fears** that stop them
- **Industry changes**, trends and debates
- The user's **point of view** and **proof**, from `profile.md`

## Quality bar

- **Short noun phrase, 1–4 words.** *Late invoices*, not *How to chase clients who pay late*. The format supplies the angle; the topic is just the subject.
- **Specific to this audience.** If a topic would fit any business's content plan unchanged (*Productivity*, *Mindset*, *Tips*), make it specific or cut it.
- **One idea per topic.** Split *Pricing and packaging* into two.
- **Mix breadth.** Include a few broad topics that many people search for alongside narrow ones that only the ideal client cares about. The narrow ones usually make the best posts.
