# Post formats

The 18 formats from the framework, grouped into four families. The family says what the post does for the reader; the balance rule in `SKILL.md` step 4 uses these families.

| Family | Does | Formats |
|---|---|---|
| **Teach** | Gives the reader something useful now | How to, Checklist, FAQ, Debunking myths, Common misconception, Red flags |
| **Story** | Builds trust through the user's experience | How I, Success story, Failure story, Transformation, Professional-personal story, Behind the scenes, Building in public |
| **Opinion** | Shows a point of view people remember | Contrary take, Disagreement, Common advice critique |
| **Proof** | Moves readers toward buying | Testimonial, Objection response |

Each format below lists what it **needs** to work. A pairing is strong when the topic supplies that need without stretching. Formats marked ★ depend on the user's real experience: use placeholders unless `profile.md` records the real story or result.

## Teach

**How to**: step-by-step guide to doing one thing.
- Needs: a task with clear steps the reader can do themselves.
- Title: *How to [outcome] without [pain]* · Hook: name the result, then the number of steps.
- Weak with: topics that are states or feelings rather than tasks (*Authenticity*).

**Checklist**: a scannable list to check against.
- Needs: 5–10 items a reader can tick off before or during a task.
- Title: *The [n]-point [moment] checklist* · Hook: the cost of missing one item.

**FAQ**: answers to the questions people actually ask.
- Needs: 3–6 real questions the audience asks about this topic.
- Title: *[Topic]: your [n] most-asked questions, answered* · Hook: the question asked most often.

**Debunking myths**: takes apart a widely held false belief.
- Needs: a belief many people in the audience hold, and evidence or experience that it's wrong.
- Title: *The [topic] myth that's costing you [x]* · Hook: state the myth as if true, then turn it.

**Common misconception**: corrects a subtle misunderstanding, gentler than a myth.
- Needs: something people half-understand, where the correction changes what they do.
- Title: *You're probably thinking about [topic] wrong* · Hook: "Most people think X. It's actually Y."

**Red flags**: warning signs to watch for.
- Needs: a decision or situation where things can go wrong in recognizable ways.
- Title: *[n] red flags when [choosing / doing X]* · Hook: the most surprising flag first.
- Weak with: purely positive or internal topics (*Small wins*).

## Story

**How I** ★: how the user did a specific thing.
- Needs: a real, specific thing the user did, with a method others can copy.
- Title: *How I [result] in [timeframe / constraint]* · Hook: the result, with one concrete number or detail.

**Success story** ★: a win, the user's own or a client's.
- Needs: a real outcome, with before, after, and what made the difference.
- Title: *What [client type] did to [result]* · Hook: the after state, then "here's what changed."

**Failure story** ★: a mistake and what it taught.
- Needs: a real mistake the user made, and a lesson the reader can apply.
- Title: *The [moment] where I [failed]* · Hook: drop the reader into the worst moment.

**Transformation** ★: change over time, from one state to another.
- Needs: a clear before and after, and the turning point between them.
- Title: *From [before] to [after]* · Hook: describe the before state vividly.

**Professional-personal story** ★: a life moment that taught a work lesson.
- Needs: a personal story the user is willing to share, with a clear bridge to the topic.
- Title: *What [personal moment] taught me about [topic]* · Hook: the personal moment, with no mention of work yet.

**Behind the scenes** ★: how the work actually gets done.
- Needs: a process, tool, decision or routine the audience doesn't normally see.
- Title: *Inside our [process]* · Hook: the detail that surprises outsiders.

**Building in public** ★: progress, numbers and decisions shared as they happen.
- Needs: an ongoing project with real updates. Works best for founders and creators; often awkward for traditional B2C or regulated businesses.
- Title: *[Project], month [n]: [what happened]* · Hook: the honest number or decision.

## Opinion

**Contrary take**: argues against the popular view.
- Needs: a mainstream view the user genuinely disagrees with (see Point of view in `profile.md`), and a reason.
- Title: *Stop [common practice]* · Hook: the bold claim in one line.

**Disagreement**: responds to a specific person, trend or piece of advice.
- Needs: a specific, recognizable view to respond to, argued respectfully.
- Title: *Why I disagree with [view]* · Hook: quote or paraphrase the view fairly, then "here's where it breaks."

**Common advice critique**: shows where standard advice falls short.
- Needs: advice the audience hears often that is incomplete or wrong for them specifically.
- Title: *"[common advice]" is only half right* · Hook: the advice, then what it leaves out.

Opinion formats only work on topics the user has a real stance on. Never manufacture disagreement; if `profile.md` has no stance on a topic, choose another format.

## Proof

**Testimonial** ★: a client's words and result.
- Needs: a real client quote or result. Always a placeholder until the user supplies one.
- Title: *What [client] said after [result]* · Hook: the quote's strongest line.
- Weak with: abstract or technical topics no client would mention (*Pauses*, *Slide design*).

**Objection response**: answers a reason people give for not buying.
- Needs: a real objection (price, time, "I can do it myself", trust) tied to this topic.
- Title: *"[objection]" — here's the honest answer* · Hook: state the objection in the audience's own words.

## Custom formats

If `map.md` lists custom formats, treat them as part of the set, each in the family that fits it best.
