# Anti-slop

AI slop is a line that's there for rhythm or to sound smart, not to say something. Readers spot it in a second, and once they do, they stop trusting the whole post.

**The test:** a list, label or short line stays only if it carries real information. The format is never the problem; the empty job is. "Colours: wrong" in a list of what came back is information. "Memory: asleep" is a joke wearing a list.

## Banned patterns

| Pattern | Slop | Fixed |
|---|---|---|
| **"Not X, it's Y"** | "It's not the team. It's 3 gaps in the workflow." | "It comes from 3 gaps in how the work moves:" |
| **A question you answer yourself** | "The irony? I teach this for a living." | "I teach this for a living. I didn't do it myself." |
| **Neat sayings** (two balanced lines that sound wise) | "Work rarely breaks in the doing. / It breaks in the handover." | End on the concrete case: "In a bakery, that one line is often an allergy. Miss it, and a customer gets the wrong cake." |
| **Three short fragments in a row** | "3 people. 1 change. 2 weeks." | "It went to 3 people, and only one of them changed anything." |
| **Labels used for rhythm** | "Reminder: none / Alarm: none / Memory: asleep" | Keep the labels that are facts, drop the joke: "Reminder: none / Alarm: none" |
| **Link lines** that only point at the next part | "Here they are:", "Same goes for deadlines, renewals…", "Good teams run the same way:" | Go straight to the list, or give the link its own concrete line. |
| **Story clichés** | "It didn't start that way.", "Then the real work began.", "So I flipped it." | Say what happened: "Before that, orders came in by email." / "So I changed who does the work:" |
| **Stock closing question** | "What's your take? Let me know below 👇" | End on the post's own last point, action or call to action. |

Also watch for: "Let that sink in", "Here's the thing", "game-changer", "unlock", "In today's fast-paced world", em-dash asides stacked in one paragraph, and a line of three adjectives.

## How to fix a slop line

**Rewrite it or turn it into a list. Never just delete it.** A slop line often holds up a list, a turn or an ending. Delete it and the post goes flat, or stops mid-thought. Ask what job the line was trying to do, and do that job plainly:

- It introduced a list → let the list's first line do the work, or write a plain one-line lead-in.
- It made a turn (problem → fix) → state the cause in plain words.
- It was the ending → end on the concrete example, the action, or the call to action.
- It packed three points into one clever line → make them a real list.

After cutting, check the post still has its shape: the lists, the short lines and the white space it had before.

**Keep the energy.** A slop line is usually there because the writer wanted punch. Replace it with something concrete that has the same punch (a real moment, a hard number, a stake), never with an explanation. "They don't have a revenue problem. They have a visibility problem." loses its slop as "I only checked the numbers at year end. A cash crunch closed the café." It doesn't lose it as "Most owners only find out if last year worked when the year-end accounts arrive." The first shows; the second explains and makes the reader work.

**Lead with the realest thing.** When a post has a real moment in it (a failure, a date, a loss, a number), that's the hook. Don't leave it in the middle as a supporting fact.

**One message.** If a fix adds a second point ("once that's a habit, add VAT and payroll too"), cut it. It's another post.

## Whole-post example

The flat version has no slop left, and no reason to keep reading:

> Many small business owners only notice late payments when cash runs short.
>
> By then, the invoices are often weeks overdue.
>
> You can avoid this by reviewing unpaid invoices each Friday:
>
> - Who owes you
> - How much
> - How many days late
>
> It takes about 15 minutes.
>
> Once that's a habit, add your upcoming bills to the review.

The strong version uses the writer's real moment as the hook, and the message lands by line 3:

> Last spring, I nearly missed payroll with £14,000 owed to me.
>
> Three clients were 60 days late. I hadn't noticed.
>
> Now, every Friday, I check one list:
>
> - Who owes me
> - How much
> - How many days late
>
> It takes 15 minutes.
>
> This Friday, open your unpaid invoices before your inbox.

(The facts here are an illustration. In a real post, the moment and the numbers must come from the user.)

## The three passes

### 1. Slop scan

Go line by line against the table above. Quote each hit, name the pattern, and fix it.

### 2. Reader pass

Read the post top to bottom as the audience would, not as its writer:

- **Message in 5 seconds:** a stranger gets the point from the first 3 lines. If they'd have to read further to know what the post is about, move the message up.
- **Feeling:** there's a real moment, stake or number that makes the reader care. A post of correct information with nothing at stake reads as having no soul.
- **Flow:** each line leads to the next. No jumps, no line that repeats the one before.
- **Finished:** the last line closes the loop the rehook opened. A post never ends on a bare list; a list at the end needs one line after it saying what to do with it.
- **Plain words:** read every word as a stranger outside the user's field. Any term they might not know (trade terms like "cash crunch", "P&L", "cash flow", "margins", "onboarding", "workflow") gets rewritten as what actually happened or what it means: "a cash crunch closed my café" becomes "we ran out of money to pay the bills, and the café closed." Words from the user's own files count too; the profile may be written in the user's shorthand.
- **Simple:** each line holds one idea. An ending that tries to say two things at once ("Put each one in the calendar the day the date is set") becomes one ("Add them to your calendar today.").
- **Not flat:** if a cleaned post reads like a block of sentences, bring back the lists and short lines that carry real information.
- **Nothing missing:** did a cut take out something the reader needed, such as a moment of the story, a step, or the reason a number matters? Cutting repeats is right; cutting beats is not. Check the length against the material (`writing-rules.md`, Length).

### 3. Truth check

Every claim about the user (a result, a number, a client, a "since 2023", "I never pitched") is backed by `profile.md`, a project proof file or the conversation. Slop often hides a small untruth: "There was never one big pitch" sounds good, but is false if the user pitched the website redesign. Rewrite to what really happened.

Take the facts from the files, not their wording. `profile.md` is notes, often in shorthand; the post says the same fact in the reader's words.

### Batch check

When there are several posts, read them side by side. If two posts use the same move (the same "What happened:" label list, the same "Same goes for…" bridge, the same kind of ending), change one of them. One post can look fine while the set looks machine-made.

## Reporting (polish mode)

For each problem: quote the line, name the pattern or failed check, and give the fix. Group by post when there are several. Then say which post is cleanest and which is worst, so the user knows where to look first.
