# Writing rules

These are the defaults. A rule in the user's `profile.md` (Voice, Avoid, Preferences) wins over anything here.

## Voice

Simple enough for a stranger, or a 5-year-old, to get the message. No jargon or buzzwords. Present tense where the story allows. Short words over long ones.

## Rules

1. **One core message.** Say it in one sentence before writing. Everything in the post serves it.
2. **Scannable, with no fixed template.** Every post: one idea per line, short lines, paragraphs of 1–2 lines with white space. Devices (bullets, numbers, timeline, label: value, →) are chosen to fit the content, so posts don't all look the same. Each list or label carries real information (`anti-slop.md`). Details in `formatting.md`.
3. **Hook, line 1.** Specific and concrete. The strongest hooks hold six things: a time, "I", a place, the work, an action and a stake ("Last month, I handed a design task to a designer."). A Teach post can start from the reader's moment instead ("Before your team spends £1 on software for next year's awards dinner,").
4. **Rehook, line 2.** Opens a loop the post closes later, so the reader keeps going (the Zeigarnik effect: people remember unfinished things). It adds tension or a question the post answers: "And one missed line doubled the work."
5. **Advice early.** If the post gives advice, put it right after the hook and rehook, not after a long build-up.
6. **Lists shaped by line length.** Readable, and ordered so the edge makes a shape: long to short, short to long, square (all about the same length), or an arrow (>), growing to the middle then shrinking. Examples in `formatting.md`.
7. **Emoji:** few, and only when they do a job (a marker, a mood the words can't carry). Follow the profile if it says none.
8. **Ending.** A real last line that comes from the post: an action the reader can take, the final point, or the call to action (Story posts: see Content types). One short idea. Vary it across posts. No stock ending, no closing question added out of habit, no sayings or punchlines, and never stop on a bare list.
9. **Every line moves forward.** Each line adds something new. Cut a line that only explains or repeats the one before. Length follows the story: long when every line earns its place, short when it doesn't need more (see Length below).

## Length

The real moments in the material decide the length. Before writing, count the beats (moments, steps, examples, facts the reader needs): that tells you roughly how long the post will be. The word counts below are a rough guide to what to expect, never a target: a post that uses every beat and comes out shorter is the right length.

| Length | Roughly (LinkedIn) | When the material has | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Short** | about 40–80 | One point and one action | A habit and the number that proves it |
| **Medium** | about 100–180 | A list with context, or a short story with a turn | A checklist with why it matters |
| **Long** | about 200–350 | A story with several moments, or a case study with its process | Before → what went wrong → what changed → result |

Use every real beat the material gives; don't drop a moment to make the post shorter. Don't add beats that aren't there to make it longer. Within a week's posts, mix the lengths.

## Content types

| Type | Formats (content-diy families) | Job | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Teach** | How to, Checklist, FAQ, Debunking myths, Common misconception, Red flags | Reach: useful to everyone in the category | A full post built on the starting post's angle and topic, applied to this audience's work. |
| **Opinion** | Contrary take, Disagreement, Common advice critique | Reach and memory | A stance the profile actually holds (Point of view), with the reason. Never invent a disagreement. |
| **Story** | How I, Failure story, Professional-personal story, Behind the scenes, Building in public, Transformation, Success story | Trust: for people who already follow | A real, everyday moment, linked back to the user's work with one concrete example from their field. |
| **Proof** | Testimonial, Objection response, Success story, Case study | Calls and sales | Sells without selling: hook → real result → a short look at the process → a soft call to action. No pitch, no price. Real results only. |

**Story posts end by context.** When the story's lesson is something the reader can do in their own work, end on that action ("Next Monday, start with last week's food cost."). When the story is about the user themself (how they started, why they do this work, what happens behind the scenes), end on the link back to their business ("My first client was the pizza place two doors down."). Pick whichever the story has earned; don't bolt an action onto an origin story, or a business plug onto a lesson.

**Story posts need the user's real moment.** If the profile and conversation don't hold one, ask for it, or write the post around a clearly marked placeholder. Don't invent a personal story.

**Proof posts need a real result.** Use the numbers and facts on record. A rounder or bigger number is a false claim.

## Platform shapes

For LinkedIn, the hook and rehook must land before "…see more" (roughly the first 2 lines, about 200 characters). For other platforms (X threads, Instagram carousels, newsletters, blogs) follow the shapes in content-diy's `references/drafting.md`, keeping every rule above.

## Visual

While a brand is starting out, every post is full written content plus one visual, and the text carries the message on its own. Short captions under a big visual work for creators with authority, not yet for a new account.

Name the visual in the draft's header: a personal photo for Story posts; an infographic, candid photo or screenshot for the rest. A visual starting post is repurposed as a visual.
