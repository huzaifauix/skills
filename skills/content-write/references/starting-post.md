# The starting post

The user brings it. Usually it's one of two things:

- **An outlier:** a post that did far better than its creator's usual (often 3× or more of their median likes). A post that beats its own creator's average shows the angle, topic or structure worked, not just the follower count.
- **An example they like the shape of:** often for Proof posts, where the posts that sell best are rarely the ones with the most likes.

## What to take from it

Read it and note:

- **Angle:** the idea that made it work ("the free version beats the paid one", "what changed in a year").
- **Topic:** what it's about, in the reader's terms.
- **Structure:** how it moves, such as hook → list → payoff, or moment → change → lesson.
- **Ending:** how it closes: a save prompt, a closing line, an action.
- **Visual:** if the post is an infographic, carousel or image, the visual is part of what worked. Repurpose it as a visual too.

Write the user's post from these notes, not from the post's sentences. Never copy its words or its length: the user's post is as long as its own material needs.

Proven posts carry slop too (a neat saying at the end, a "repost if…" line, an emoji). Take what made the post work, and leave its slop behind; the polish in step 4 would catch it anyway.

## A good Proof example

A post that sells without selling:

- a hook,
- a real result,
- a short look at the process behind it,
- a soft call to action.

No pitch and no price. If the example the user brings leans salesy, keep its shape and drop the push.

## When the starting post can't be used

If it doesn't fit any pillar or topic in `map.md`, or its angle needs a story or result the user doesn't have, say so in one line and suggest the closest fit, rather than forcing it.
